## A.2 The Elevator Pitch

You start with $50,000, a lanyard, and a questionnaire. You build businesses — any businesses: AI startups, hedge funds, fast-food empires, *plumbing* — in Liquidity City, a cartoon coastal metropolis where everything is for sale and the leaderboard is carved into the tallest building. Up to fifteen other players share your economy: undercut them, merge with them, sit on their boards, sabotage their supply lines, race their yachts. Claw your way up the net-worth ladder toward the one number nobody has ever hit: **$1,000,000,000,000**.

There's just one thing. Nobody crosses a billion alone. At $999,999,999 your deals start falling through — until a stranger at a gala hands you a phone with a cheerful clicker game already installed, and the crab mascot reads you the terms. Capital beyond imagination, in exchange for a modest recurring commitment: **your soul works the night shift.**

It's *Succession* meets *Saints Row* meets first contact. The satire is the sandbox. The horror is the fine print.

## A.3 The Twist (spoilers — narrative team and marketing beat-planning only)

> The game presents as a pure wealth-race sim for its first act. It is actually a Faustian cosmic-horror comedy: an alien entity called **The Auditors** — a civilization that long ago *became* an economy — quietly underwrites every fortune above $1B on Earth. They cannot want; they harvest human *wanting*, refined through contracted souls who labor in a wrong-copy of the city called **The Adjacent Office** while their bodies sleep. Becoming the trillionaire means completing their acquisition. The full lore, pact mechanics, story beats, and four endings are specified in Part D. The elites aren't controlling the world — something is leasing it *through* them, and the players are next in line. No real-world politics, parties, or persons are referenced anywhere; the only conspiracy in this game is extraterrestrial and it has a compliance department.

# PART B — CHARACTER CREATION & CLASSES

*Design goal: Skyrim's emergent-class philosophy wearing a business-satire suit. Players never pick a class from a list. They answer questions, allocate skills, choose traits and an origin — and the game deduces what kind of mogul they are, then announces it to the world. Your class is the industry you were always going to dominate.*

## B.1 The Intake (creation flow, 8–12 minutes, fully diegetic)

Character creation is an in-fiction scene: a beige, fluorescent office — **LiquidCorp Onboarding Solutions** — where a smiling proctor walks you through the **Wealth Aptitude Questionnaire**. Five stations:

1. **The Mirror** — body/face/voice customization. Cartoony proportion sliders (chunky Fortnite-adjacent stylization), 40+ preset faces, full wardrobe. Satirical options are mechanical-neutral cosmetics: *Jawline (Sponsored)*, *Haircut: "I Own a Submarine,"* *Posture: Board-Ready*.
2. **The Paperwork** — pick an **Origin** (B.2). Presented as a résumé you assemble from lies and truths.
3. **The Assessment** — allocate 60 points across ten **Skills** (B.4), via absurd aptitude minigames (a Rorschach test where every answer is "profit," a marshmallow test with a hostile-takeover option).
4. **The Interview** — pick three **Traits** (B.3) by answering the proctor's increasingly strange questions. (*"You find a wallet on the street. How much do you charge to return it?"*)
5. **The Signature** — sign your name (the physical scribble minigame from Part C §1.4 — signature quality is set here and matters forever). You are handed a lanyard, $50,000 of seed capital, and shown the door into Liquidity City.

**The long-game detail (do not cut):** the Intake's questionnaire IS the Auditors' data collection. In Act II (Part D §D.4), the player's pact contract appendix reproduces *their own creation-screen answers*, verbatim, dated before they "started playing." The character creator was first contact.

## B.5 The Christening (emergent class assignment)

At the moment you found your first business (Part C §2.2), the game evaluates **top two Skills + Origin + Traits + chosen industry** and christens you — full-screen magazine cover, *LIQUIDITY WEEKLY* declaring the world's newest **[CLASS]**, your face, a headline generated from your creation answers. Your class is announced to the whole session. This is your build reveal and your gossip moment in one.

Class determines: a **signature ability**, a **passive**, an **affinity bonus** in your home industry, unique cosmetic gear, and a class questline. Classes map to the twelve industries (Part C §2.2 table holds the underlying economy stats).

## B.6 Progression: Millionaire → Billionaire → Trillionaire-Candidate

- **Portfolios (perk trees):** each Skill's tree is drawn as an org chart; perks are "hires" into your personal brain-C-suite. ~200 perks at launch. Sample picks: *Vision 30 — Weaponized Vaporware* (announce an unbuilt product; it earns Hype as if real for 48h, then must ship or meme-debuff); *Legalese 50 — Actually Read It* (all contracts display one hidden clause, including Bridge Rounds); *Craft 70 — Honest Work* (supply nodes you repair personally stay green 50% longer); *Taste 90 — Curator's Eye* (see an artwork's true resale range at auction).
- **Wealth gates do the class-fantasy pacing:** the net-worth Milestone Ladder (Part C §5.2) unlocks tiers of traversal, business scale, and districts' inner sanctums — progression is the city itself opening up.
- **The pact tier (post-$1B) — Golden Handcuffs:** signing (mandatory to pass $1B; Part C §5.2) opens a fourth perk category, blessed-and-cursed: *Compound Interest* (liquid cash +0.5%/day; your reflection visibly counts it), *Delegated Conscience* (scandals resolve 50% faster; your Night Shift copy files the paperwork), *Billable Dreams* (sleep grants a working-hours buff; Night Shift chance +15%). Every Golden Handcuffs perk raises lifetime Soul Debt exposure — power now, priced later (Part C §5.5).

## 1. THE WORLD

### 1.1 The City: LIQUIDITY CITY, capital of the Solvent Coast

A coastal mega-city built by, for, and about money. Every skyline sightline terminates in something for sale. The city's motto, embossed on every manhole cover: **"ALL ASSETS APPRECIATED."**

**Map facts:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Playable area | 9.6 km² land + 4.2 km² ocean/harbor ("The Bay of Liquidity") |
| Vertical range | -80 m (bunker/subway strata) to +610 m (Needle of Valuation rooftop) |
| Districts | 12 surface districts + 2 hidden strata |
| Enterable interiors at launch | 140+ (every player-ownable business has a walkable interior) |
| NPC ambient population | 300–600 streamed pedestrians/vehicles per player cluster |
| Day/night cycle | 48 real minutes = 24 in-game hours (economy ticks hourly, in-game) |

**Design pillar:** the map is a *portfolio you can walk through*. Every district is simultaneously (a) a themed playground, (b) an industry cluster for the business sim, and (c) a satire target. Players should be able to point at any building and ask "can I buy that?" and the answer is yes, eventually, at an insulting price.

**Hidden strata:**

- **The Understack** (-10 m to -80 m): service tunnels, doomsday bunkers, the private subway for players worth $10B+ ("The Pneumatic Club"). Also where sabotage gameplay physically happens — cut a rival's fiber line in the tunnels under their HQ.

### 1.3 The Alien Layer (world-integrated eeriness)

The aliens — in-fiction called **The Auditors** — never appear as creatures in the open world. Their presence is environmental, Three-Body-style: pervasive, intelligent, unexplained.

- **Surveillance motif:** every reflective surface in Liquidity City is an audit. At Soul Debt ≥ 40 (see 5.5), the player's reflection lags behind them by 200 ms. At ≥ 70, the reflection sometimes finishes the player's actions *first*.
- **Numeric intrusions:** ambient numbers (billboards, gas prices, the Ticker crawl) occasionally flicker to impossible values — a stock price of "YES," a clock showing 25:61 — visible only to pact-holders. Other players just see you staring at a normal billboard.
- **The second moon:** appears only in mirrors, puddles, and yacht-hull reflections. Never in the sky. Never acknowledged by NPCs.
- **Game-within-game channel:** all Auditor communication arrives through **CLAWS & CLAUSES**, an idle clicker game that self-installs on the player's in-game phone after their first pact. Its "levels" are contract negotiations; its "daily rewards" are demands; its cheerful cartoon crab mascot reads terms aloud in a flat, wrong cadence. Uninstalling it reinstalls it. The install size grows by 1 byte every real day.
- **Physics courtesies:** completing an Auditor demand triggers a 10-second window where local physics get *polite* — dropped champagne glasses land upright, dice roll your way, ragdolls bow. Unsettling, never explained, mechanically a small luck buff (+15% minigame RNG for 10 min).

### 1.4 First-Person Feel (cartoon body, real presence)

Target: GTA-first-person freedom with Fortnite-adjacent stylization — chunky proportions, saturated palette, exaggerated squash-and-stretch on the world, but a **grounded camera** so the sim reads as embodied.

- **Camera:** default 95° FOV (75–110 slider), full-body awareness (look down, see your ludicrous outfit), procedural head-bob ≤ 1.5 cm amplitude with an off toggle, contextual camera pushes (crowds part for you above $1B net worth — the camera subtly widens as NPCs clear a radius proportional to wealth: 1 m per $10B, capped at 6 m; this is both a joke and a readable status display).
- **Hands are the UI:** phone, pen (contract signing is a physical scribble minigame), champagne flute, keynote clicker. Signature quality on contracts actually matters: a sloppy signature has a 5% chance to be "misfiled," delaying the deal one in-game day. Rich-people paperwork as a dexterity toy.
- **Movement:** walk 2.2 m/s, "power-walk" 4.5 m/s (arms pump, NPCs get out of the way), sprint 7 m/s, slide, mantle, ledge-grab. No fall damage — instead, falls over 12 m trigger an **Insurance Event**: a lawyer NPC teleports in, catches you in a comically small airbag, and bills you 0.01% of net worth. Consequence-free for the body, never for the wallet. That's the satire and the money-sink in one system.
- **Traversal ladder (unlock by net worth — wealth literally changes how you move through the world):**

| Tier | Net worth gate | Traversal unlocks | Top speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aspirant | $0+ | Rideshare, e-scooter, used sedan | 22 m/s |
| Millionaire | $1M | Sports cars, jet-ski, Segway-with-butler | 45 m/s |
| Centimillionaire | $100M | Helicopter, speedboat, horse (imported, apologetic) | 60 m/s |
| Billionaire | $1B | **Gold Hoverboard** (22 m/s, tricks grant Ego, leaves a trail of depreciating confetti-cash cosmetically only), private heli with hot tub, submarine | 80 m/s (heli) |
| Decabillionaire | $10B | Pneumatic Club subway (fast-travel between all districts, 8 s ride, mandatory jazz), VTOL "Ego-Jet" | Fast travel |
| Centibillionaire | $100B | **Suborbital Hop** — personal rocket taxi, launches from any helipad, 30 s ballistic arc to anywhere on the map with a skydive re-entry on your hoverboard | Map-wide in 30 s |

- **Vehicles handle cartoony-arcade** (forgiving drift, bouncy collisions, horns play licensed-sounding-but-legally-distinct yacht rock), but **damage bills are real**: repair costs scale with vehicle price, so the $80M Ego-Jet is genuinely stressful to fly through a billboard. Wealth insulates you from injury but never from invoices.
- **Traffic & crowd tech note:** pedestrian archetypes are reactive satire — they photograph players above $1B, ask for autographs, or (if your public Reputation is low) throw legally-distinct milkshakes that only stain cosmetics for 5 minutes.

## 2. THE ECONOMY & BUSINESS SIM

### 2.1 Design pillars

1. **Tycoon depth, first-person verbs.** Every spreadsheet number has a place you can physically stand. Factory throughput is a dial in a room, not a menu (there is also a menu, for remote management — but on-site management gives a +10% "Founder's Presence" buff for 2 in-game hours after a walkthrough).
2. **Every industry is viable.** A plumbing empire can beat an AI empire. Industries differ in *shape* of income (volatility, margin, scandal-exposure, alien-interest), not in ceiling.
3. **Satire through mechanics.** The systems themselves are the jokes: layoffs boost your stock price but spawn picketing ex-mascots outside your HQ who slow foot traffic (–8% retail revenue) until you rehire or out-PR them. Nothing is preachy; everything is a lever with a funny consequence.

### 2.2 Founding a business: the LLC-in-a-Box

Any player can found a business at any **District Registry Kiosk** (or via phone at a 5% "convenience fee" — laziness tax).

Founding flow (3–5 minutes):
1. **Pick industry** (one of 12 clusters, matching districts; a business founded in its home district gets +10% synergy revenue).
2. **Pick a physical lot** from available parcels (14–30 per district; parcels are per-player instanced ownership in freeroam, contested only through the Takeover system — no "someone bought the whole map" dead states).
3. **Name it** (profanity-filtered; the name generator offers 100% satirical suggestions: "Grift & Sons," "Definitely Not A Pyramid LLC," "Moist Ventures").
4. **Seed capital:** minimum buy-in per tier (below).
5. **Hire a founding executive** (see 2.4).

**Business tiers & economy targets:**

| Tier | Buy-in | Gross revenue/in-game hr (baseline) | Staff (NPC) | Walkable interior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Hustle | $50K | $2K–$8K | 1–4 | Kiosk/storefront |
| T2 Company | $2M | $40K–$150K | 10–40 | Small building |
| T3 Corporation | $80M | $1.5M–$6M | 100–400 | HQ + 1 facility |
| T4 Conglomerate | $2B | $30M–$120M | 1,000+ | Campus, multiple sites |
| T5 Monopoly-in-all-but-name | $50B | $500M–$2B | "We stopped counting" | District-scale footprint |

A player can own up to **12 businesses** simultaneously (raised to 16 via a Life Goal). Each additional business past 3 adds management overhead: without a hired executive, an unattended business decays −2% revenue/in-game day.

**Income shape by industry (the strategic personality of each class):**

| Industry | Margin | Volatility | Scandal risk | Auditor interest | Signature mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tech/AI | High | High | Medium | **Very high** | Keynote launches spike revenue ×3 for 24 hrs if performed well (see Activities) |
| Crypto | Absurd | **Extreme** | High | High | Coin value tied to your live behavior — crashing your own yacht dips your coin 12% |
| Retail/Logistics | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | Volume king: revenue scales with delivery-network coverage you physically build |

### 2.3 Supply chains (physical, sabotage-able)

T3+ businesses have 2–4 **supply nodes** rendered in-world: a warehouse, a fiber line, a pipeline, a server room, a shipping lane buoy. Each node has an uptime state that multiplies revenue (all-green = 100%, each red node −20%).

- Nodes are maintained by assigning executives or paying a standing **OpEx** fee (1–4% of revenue).
- Nodes are the **legitimate target surface for PvP sabotage** (2.6) — attacking a node is gameplay (stealth/heist verbs), never destroys the business, and auto-repairs in 2 in-game days or after a repair mission.
- Vertical integration: owning your supplier industry (e.g., logistics + retail) converts one node to permanently green. This quietly teaches conglomerate strategy.

### 2.4 NPC Executives (your middle management of the damned)

Hire from the **Executive Poaching Lounge** (a bar in The Vault where CVs float in cocktail glasses). Executives are procedurally generated from archetype pools — the satire's supporting cast:

- **Stats:** Competence (1–10), Loyalty (1–10), Ambition (1–10), Scandal Coefficient (0.0–1.0).
- **Archetypes:** The Grindset Guy (high competence, posts motivational quotes that occasionally leak trade secrets), The Legacy Hire (low competence, +Reputation with old money), The Turnaround Artist (boosts failing businesses +30%, then demands your job), The Extremely Normal Person (perfectly average, secretly your best hire, the game never tells you this).
- **Mechanics:** an executive assigned to a business removes decay and adds Competence×2% revenue. High Ambition + low Loyalty = 5%/week chance they attempt an internal coup (a defend-your-boardroom social minigame). Scandal Coefficient rolls weekly against Media pressure; a fired scandal-executive can be **poached by rival players**, carrying one piece of intel about your supply nodes with them. Hiring is therefore also counterintelligence.
- **Salary:** 0.5–3% of the business's revenue. You can underpay; underpaid executives' Loyalty decays 1/week. The game never editorializes; the numbers just happen to you.

### 2.5 THE TICKER (stock-market minigame)

The in-world exchange, physically located in The Vault (tradeable anywhere by phone at worse spreads — being on the floor gives 0.2% better fills, so the pit stays socially alive).

- **Universe:** ~60 NPC stocks (satirical: BLORP Industries, YachtCo, Definitely Fine Bank) + **every player-owned T3+ business that chooses to IPO**.
- **IPO:** a T3+ owner can float 10–49% of a business. Instant capital injection = valuation × float %. Cost: your revenue is now public, you owe quarterly **Earnings Calls** (a live 90-second performance minigame — confidence stat + prepared "guidance" choices; a botched call drops your stock 5–15%), and other players can buy your shares (see board seats, 3.3).
- **Price model:** baseline drift + revenue-linked fundamentals + session-event noise + player order flow. Deliberately arcade-legible: candles update every 30 real seconds; max position sizes scale with net worth to keep whales from deleting the market for a 4-player session.
- **Order verbs:** buy, sell, short (borrow fee 0.5%/in-game day, liquidation at −80%), and **Options Lite** ("Calls" and "Puts" as fixed-price scratch-cards with 3× payout — satirically labeled "Gambling, Legally Distinct From").
- **Insider Whispers:** doing activities in the world generates private info — overhear an executive at a gala, read documents during a heist, notice unusual truck volume at a rival's warehouse (the world-sim actually renders extra trucks before an earnings beat). Trading on Whispers is legal in Liquidity City (the joke) but flags you to **the SEC-adjacent entity, "The Commission of Vibes,"** whose only power is a public Reputation hit and a very disappointed press conference about you.
- **Market events** (1–3 per real hour, session-wide, announced via news chyrons): Rate Vibes Shift (finance ±), Chip Shortage (tech −, trades +), Health Craze (fast food −, biotech +), **"The Numbers Are Wrong" (Auditor event: all prices display scrambled for 90 seconds; trades still execute at true prices — a pure nerve test)**.

### 2.6 Acquisition, Sabotage, Hostile Takeovers

Three escalating ways to eat a rival (NPC-owned or player-owned):

1. **Friendly Acquisition** — offer ≥ 1.3× valuation. NPC owners accept via a negotiation minigame (golf, sauna, or omakase — venue choice matters: each NPC archetype has a preferred venue worth +15% acceptance). Player owners just get a real offer UI.
2. **Sabotage** (players only, flagged-PvP consent rules in 3.4) — stealth missions against supply nodes: cut fiber in the Understack, bribe a mascot to "accidentally" bad-review a chain, redirect delivery drones with a signal spoofer. Effects are temporary (−20% revenue per node, 2 in-game days), always traceable with effort (victims can run a **Forensic Audit** mission to unmask the attacker for a Reputation bounty), and never delete progress. Sabotage lowers a target's valuation, setting up step 3.
3. **Hostile Takeover** (the crown-jewel PvP loop, 20–40 min): only against IPO'd businesses. Attacker secretly accumulates shares (buying >5% in a day triggers a public "Whale Alert" — stealth-buying via shell companies costs a 10% premium). At 30%+ attacker can call a **Board Vote**: a multi-stage social/action event — sway NPC board members (each has a price, a fear, or a hobby), win a live proxy-battle debate minigame, while the defender counters with poison pills (expensive: 5% of net worth), white-knight alliances (another player buys in defensively), or simply better numbers. Victory transfers the business at market price (defender is paid — takeovers redistribute, never destroy). Losing a takeover you initiated costs your stake plus a Reputation hit. Expected frequency tuning: a 16-player session should see 1–3 takeover attempts per multi-hour session, not constant warfare.

### 2.7 Money sinks (mandatory, or the numbers break)

Trillion-scale economies die without drains. Sinks are the lifestyle content itself: mansion upkeep (0.1%/week of property value), staff wages, yacht harbor fees, vehicle repair, Insurance Events, gala hosting costs, executive salaries, the **Wealth Ambience Fee** (above $10B, the city bills you for the crowd control, red carpets, and camera drones that follow you everywhere — 0.05% of net worth weekly, itemized hilariously: "Confetti: $1.2M"). Target macro: 20–30% of session GDP recycled through sinks.

### 2.8 Net-Worth Leaderboard

**Net Worth = liquid cash + (equity × live Ticker price) + appraised assets (properties, vehicles, art) + private-business valuation (8× hourly revenue × tier multiplier) − debt − Reputational Liability (a dollar-ized penalty from active scandals, 0–5% drag).**

Displayed: session leaderboard (live, on the side of the Needle of Valuation in 40-meter numerals — your rank is literal civic architecture) and the **Global Ledger** (cross-session, account-level; see 3.5). Updates every 60 real seconds.

## 3. MULTIPLAYER STRUCTURE

### 3.1 Sessions

- **1–16 players**, drop-in/drop-out, GTA Online-shaped: Public (matchmade), Friends/Crew, Invite-Only, and **Solo** (full progression parity — see 3.6).
- One shared open world per session; missions and interiors instance seamlessly (walking into your boardroom for a story mission fades others out; freeroam activities stay shared-world).
- **Session economy is shared:** one Ticker, one market-event stream, one net-worth board per session. Sixteen moguls in one economy is the core multiplayer fantasy — your rivals are your market conditions.

### 3.2 Freeroam

Default state. All Activities (Section 4) launchable from world locations. Ambient PvP-lite systems always on: leaderboard posturing, Whisper-gathering on each other, competitive Auction House drops (a rare painting or a unique parcel goes up every ~40 min — open ascending auction, 3 minutes, held physically in The Vault so you can watch rivals sweat).

### 3.3 Co-op Ventures

- **Joint Corporations:** 2–8 players found a business with an explicit equity split (set at founding, renegotiable only unanimously). Revenue auto-splits. Joint corps get a +15% revenue bonus per additional founder (capped +45%) — cooperation is mathematically encouraged. One shared HQ all founders can decorate (and passive-aggressively re-decorate).
- **Board Seats:** own ≥ 10% of a rival's IPO'd company and you get a literal chair in their boardroom, attendance rights at their earnings calls, a vote in takeover events, and — the fun part — a **Motion** once per week (propose a dividend, a name change, a mascot). Frenemy infrastructure: your co-op partner today is one share purchase away from being on your board tomorrow.
- **Co-op missions:** Space launches (4-player launch-control roles), gala heists (2–4), mega-mergers (2 players fuse T4 businesses into a T5 via a joint mission chain), and Auditor demands explicitly built for shared completion (3.4 of Section 5).

### 3.4 PvP (structured + consensual)

- **Structured modes** (lobby-launched from world landmarks): Hostile Takeovers (2.6), **Smear Campaign Wars** (media-industry PvP: 15-minute asymmetric mode — attacker plants scandal stories via minigames, defender runs damage-control; stakes are Reputation and stock price, never property), **Yacht Regatta** (racing with wake physics and legal-but-immoral shortcut bribes), **Keynote Clash** (two players launch competing products simultaneously; the session's NPC consumers — and spectating players — vote with purchases), **Bunker Ball** (arena sport played in doomsday bunkers, betting-enabled).
- **Open-world sabotage** requires PvP-flag consent: both parties must be **"Publicly Traded"** (a toggleable stance) — you cannot be sabotaged unless you've IPO'd something, and IPO explicitly warns you that going public makes you a target. Private-company players are mechanically boring but safe: the risk/reward satire of going public.
- **Anti-grief:** no player can be killed (combat is slapstick-nonlethal: golden briefcase melees, champagne-cork snipers; "defeat" = your lawyer Insurance Event teleports you out, small cash bill). No progress destruction anywhere — all PvP redistributes or temporarily debuffs. Persistent harassment throttle: the same attacker can hit the same victim's nodes max once per real hour. **Litigation Shield:** pay 0.1% net worth per day to become sabotage-immune — the "rich people escape consequences via lawyers" joke as a passive-mode mechanic; while shielded you also *cannot* sabotage, and a giant holographic lawyer visibly orbits you (shame as balancing).

### 3.5 Persistence & Cross-Session Net Worth

- **Account-level Global Ledger** (server-authoritative): your net worth, businesses, properties, executives, Life Goals, and Soul Debt persist across all sessions. Businesses generate offline revenue at 25% rate (capped at 24 hours of accrual) so the sim breathes without demanding daily logins.
- **Session-local layer:** Ticker prices, market events, auction items, and takeover states are per-session. Your *holdings* travel; the *market* is local. (Your shares in another player's company convert to a bond-like instrument when you're in different sessions — paying fixed interest — and reactivate as voting equity when you share a session. This keeps board-seat gameplay session-based while investment value persists.)
- **Fiscal Years (seasons, ~10 weeks):** the Global Ledger's *race* leaderboard resets each Fiscal Year (see 5.3 for why), but assets never reset — instead, veterans' old wealth converts partly to **Legacy** (a prestige currency buying cosmetics, mansion wings, and museum exhibits about yourself), keeping the trillionaire race winnable by new players each season without wiping anyone's toys.

### 3.6 Solo Viability (a hard requirement)

- Every system has an NPC-driven mirror: **The House Moguls** — 6 persistent AI billionaires, one per major archetype (the Rocket Messiah, the Everything-Store Emperor, the Hedge-Fund Vampire, the Wellness Guru-Empress, the Crypto Boy-King, the Quiet Plumbing Magnate who is somehow always #2 on the leaderboard). They IPO, take over, sabotage (telegraphed and fair), throw galas, and trash-talk via the in-game news. A solo session is a full 7-mogul economy.
- All co-op missions have solo scaling (NPC crew fills roles at 85% efficiency; the launch-control AI copilot is voiced as a deeply tired middle manager).
- Solo and multiplayer progression rates are within ±10% of each other by design; multiplayer's edge is social (joint-corp bonuses, player-market liquidity), never a gate.

## 4. THE "LOT TO DO" LIST

Thirty distinct activities. Each is a repeatable system with its own economy hook (payout/cost), not a one-off scene. Grouped by fantasy.

**MOGUL WORK (core loops):**
1. **Board Meetings** — weekly per T3+ business; a seated social-deduction minigame (spot which executive is lying about their numbers). Skippable, but attending grants +5% revenue for the week.
2. **Product Launch Keynotes** — stage-performance minigame at the Amphitheater: timing applause lines, demoing a product that may or may not work live (risk slider: safer demo = smaller spike; "it just works" bravado = ×3 revenue or a market-wide meme at your expense that debuffs you 24 hrs).
3. **Earnings Calls** — quarterly public-company performance (2.5).
4. **Factory Floor Walks** — first-person inspection of your facilities: find 5 inefficiencies (physical objects: a jammed conveyor, a napping mascot, a whiteboard that just says "SYNERGY?") for the Founder's Presence buff.

6. **Tax-Haven Paperwork Speedrun** — a pure absurdist timed dexterity gauntlet at the "Office of Extremely Legal Structures": physically carry documents through a bureaucratic obstacle course of stamps, faxes, and a notary who moves at 0.1 m/s; leaderboard-timed; payout is a 24-hr sink-fee reduction, satire is the sweat.
7. **The Ticker floor trading** — open-outcry pit sessions with better spreads (2.5).
8. **Forensic Audit** — counter-sabotage investigation missions (2.6).

**LIFESTYLE (sinks with gameplay):**

11. **Vanity Submarine Program** — fund, design (comically, safety sliders cost extra and default to zero), and pilot submersibles to Bay-floor wreck dives; deep-sea exploration content with loot (lost cargo, "abstract sculpture, provenance unclear," Auditor artifacts below 200 m).

20. **Drive-Thru Grand Prix** — timed circuit through Golden Sprawl ordering at every franchise; eat-while-driving handling debuff stacking.
21. **Gold Hoverboard Trick Lines** — skate-game scoring across district-specific lines; Ego payouts.

## 5. LIFE GOALS SYSTEM & THE ROAD TO ONE TRILLION

### 5.1 Structure

Two interleaved tracks the player climbs simultaneously:

- **Net-Worth Milestones** — hard wealth gates ($1M → $1T) that unlock traversal, business tiers, and districts' inner sanctums.
- **Elite Life Goals (ELGs)** — 120+ absurd achievement-quests in five tiers (Gauche → Tasteful → Obscene → Mythic → Post-Material), each granting **Ego** (the prestige/XP currency: buys ELG-locked cosmetics, mansion wings, the right to add your face to the Needle of Valuation for one real hour) and, critically, **Audit Credits** (see 5.5).

Sample ELGs per tier: *Gauche* — own 10 cars you never drive; *Tasteful* — host a gala where nothing goes wrong (harder than it sounds; the game ensures something wants to go wrong); *Obscene* — own a board seat on every House Mogul's company simultaneously; *Mythic* — win a hostile takeover, a regatta, and a keynote clash in one session ("The Hat Trick of Capital"); *Post-Material* — reach $100B with a plumbing-only portfolio ("Mario Was Right").

### 5.2 The Milestone Ladder

| Milestone | Unlock | The Catch (introduced here) |
|---|---|---|
| $1M | Millionaire traversal tier; T2 businesses | A stranger at a gala hands you a phone with CLAWS & CLAUSES pre-installed. "It's a fun little game," they say, not blinking enough. |
| $100M | T3, IPO rights, Heritage Hills parcels | The crab mascot offers your first **Bridge Round** (below). Declinable. For now, everything is declinable. |
| $1B | Gold Hoverboard; Billionaire's Row; the leaderboard shows your name on the Needle | **You cannot cross $1B without a signed Pact.** Organic wealth stalls at $999,999,999 — deals mysteriously fall through, a decimal point goes missing, your IPO underprices by exactly the shortfall. The game makes it clear: *no one* on the Global Ledger above $1B is unsigned. This is the twist landing as a mechanic. |
| $10B | Pneumatic Club; T5 businesses; House Moguls treat you as a peer (their sabotage AI activates) | First mandatory Compliance Event. Soul Debt meter becomes visible. |
| $100B | Suborbital Hop; the endgame arc "The Final Audit" begins | Night Shifts become weekly. Your reflection has opinions. |
| $999.99B | — | The last $10M cannot be earned. It can only be **granted or seized** (5.6). |
| **$1,000,000,000,000** | **FIRST TRILLIONAIRE — Fiscal Year victory** | — |

### 5.3 The Race (competitive framing)

- Within a session: live leaderboard rivalry, continuous.
- Across the game: each **Fiscal Year** (season), the first account to verified $1T wins that Fiscal Year — a global, server-wide race with a public countdown site energy (in-game news tracks the top 100 daily). Winner immortalization: statue in The Vault for the following season, unique Legacy title, and their portfolio is converted to Legacy as they "ascend" (a soft prestige-reset that keeps the next race open). Everyone else keeps building; wealth persists, only the *crown* resets.
- **Catch-up mechanics:** below-median players get "Underdog Whispers" (better insider info frequency) and discounted Bridge Rounds — thematically, the Auditors prefer hungry clients.

### 5.4 The Pact in Play: Bridge Rounds & Night Shift

- **Bridge Rounds:** at each wealth gate, the Auditors offer capital/impossibilities ("your factory now completes Tuesday's work on Monday") in exchange for **Soul Debt** (+10 to +25 per round). Text of every offer is delivered via CLAWS & CLAUSES in cheerful clicker-game UI over terms that read like cosmic horror run through a compliance department: *"Section 4(b): the Client's sleep shall be considered billable."*
- **Night Shift** (the soul-labor gameplay): whenever the player sleeps (voluntary time-skip/save) with Soul Debt > 0, there is a Debt%-scaled chance they wake in **The Adjacent Office** — a wrong-copy of Liquidity City where they perform tasks whose purpose is never explained and always *almost* legible: alphabetize a warehouse of doors; hold a phone until it stops ringing (it does not stop ringing; the task ends anyway); walk a factory floor identical to your own where the product is a sound. 5–8 minute sequences, genuinely eerie, mordantly funny, mechanically rewarded (Soul Debt −5, plus a "Well Rested (Legally)" buff) so players are complicit in choosing them. Fragments of what the Auditors *are* leak only here, in wrong details: the break-room posters count down instead of up; your coworkers are other players' characters, idle-animating, who do not respond and later have no memory of it (they were never really there — or your session's copies weren't).
- **Compliance Events (timed sandbox invasions):** above $10B, demands arrive with real-time deadlines (20–60 min), always executable through the sandbox's own systems — the aliens' motives are incomprehensible but their asks are *your gameplay*: "Ship 40,000 tons of Grade-C gravel to open-ocean coordinates by 06:00" (logistics), "One million residents must hum the same jingle simultaneously" (media empire + keynote), "Ensure the number 7 does not appear on the Ticker for one hour" (market manipulation as ritual), "Sleep. Just sleep. Please." (the scariest one). Compliance pays enormous, milestone-critical sums and −Soul Debt; refusal is allowed and priced: +Soul Debt, a temporary "Audit Hold" freezing 10% of liquid cash, and physics getting *impolite* around you (−15% minigame RNG) — the inverse courtesy. Some demands are flagged **Joint Liability**: 2–4 pact-holders in a session must cooperate, including rivals. Nothing forges a temporary alliance like a shared creditor from outside spacetime.

### 5.5 Soul Debt (0–100)

The second resource of the endgame. Rises with Bridge Rounds and refusals; falls with Night Shifts, Compliance, and **Audit Credits** (earned only via ELGs — the design's thesis in a mechanic: *the absurd human joy content is the only thing that buys your soul back*). Escalating effects at 25/50/75: reflection lag, NPC dialogue occasionally addressed to someone behind you, your character's idle animations replaced by too-still standing, and at 75+, brief seconds where you can *hear the Ticker thinking*.

### 5.6 Endgame: The Final Audit (win condition fork)

At $999.99B, the last $10M is locked behind a choice delivered as the final level of CLAWS & CLAUSES:

- **DELIVER:** fulfill the Pact's final clause — a multi-stage mission chain using every system you've built (your logistics ships it, your media covers it up, your rockets lift it) to hand the Auditors their true acquisition (kept ambiguous; the documents redact themselves as you read them). You become the First Trillionaire. Your soul's contract is marked "RETAINED IN PERPETUITY." Victory screen; your character's reflection does not appear in the trophy.
- **DEFAULT:** breach the contract — a heist against the Adjacent Office itself, repossessing your soul (and optionally, in 4-player co-op, your partners'). You forfeit alien-granted wealth (dropping to whatever fraction you built through *organic* businesses — the plumbing magnates smile) and win nothing this Fiscal Year, but earn the game's rarest permanent title, massive Legacy, and an ending where your reflection finally waves back.
- Both endings are replayable across Fiscal Years; the leaderboard records which kind of trillionaire — or non-trillionaire — you chose to be. The race crowns the fastest. The Ledger remembers everything else.

### 5.7 Loop Summary (one paragraph a producer can pin to a wall)

Minute-to-minute: walk your world, run a minigame, harvest a Whisper. Hour-to-hour: grow businesses, trade the Ticker, do three Activities, jockey the session leaderboard. Session-to-session: climb milestones, sign Bridge Rounds, pay for them at night. Season-to-season: race sixteen-player economies and a whole server to one trillion dollars, and decide — mechanically, personally, hilariously — what it actually costs.

# PART D — THE ALIEN PACT & NARRATIVE

*Spoiler tier: full. This part is the game's secret spine. In marketing, the aliens do not exist.*

## D.1 The Auditors

**What they are.** Not invaders. Not observers. A civilization that, eons ago, optimized itself so completely that it *became* an economy — individuals merged into instruments, culture liquidated into liquidity, desire itself amortized away. They are perfectly solvent and incapable of wanting. Cosmic horror, one line: **the universe has an economy, and Earth has just been listed as an emerging market.**

**How they perceive us.** Only through transactions. A human who never buys or sells is literally invisible to them (a late-game lore thread: the one NPC in Liquidity City the Auditors cannot see is a street musician who only barters — finding him is a hidden quest). Prices are their language; markets are their sense organs. Their first message to humanity was not a signal: it was a Tuesday on which every vending machine on Earth gave exact change.

**What they need.** *Wanting.* Refined human desire — they call it **Appreciation**. Concentrated belief is the only commodity they cannot mint, because minting requires wanting the result. Money is humanity's densest belief-artifact, and a trillionaire is a lens focusing eight billion people's belief into a single number. The Auditors underwrite Earth's ultra-rich the way we run orchards: the first *verified* trillion-dollar fortune, grown from genuine striving, is a redeemable instrument in whatever market exists beyond ours (contracts refer to it only as **the Interbank Dark**). You are not the customer. You are not even the product. You are the *mint*.

**Three-Body-grade machinery (use these; they are the sci-fi meat):**
- **The Decimals** — sophon-analog observers that reside inside decimal points. Every price display in the world is a camera. This is why Compliance demands can be things like "the number 7 must not appear on the Ticker for one hour" — instrument hygiene.
- **Market-speak** — Auditors answer questions only through price movements. A late-game system lets high-Legalese players *ask them things* by structuring trades, and read replies in the candles.
- **Accounting physics** — their cosmology treats conservation laws as double-entry bookkeeping. A Night Shift poster reads: *"MATTER IS WHAT ENERGY USES FOR COLLATERAL."* None of this is ever explained further.
- **Fiscal time** — they experience time in quarters, all at once. The Concierge (D.3) refers to your future in the past tense, and is never wrong.

## D.2 The Pact

**The mechanic in fiction.** A Bridge Round (Part C §5.4) is signed with a physical pen, in first person, with your creation-scene signature. The contract is thousands of pages that read as cosmic horror run through a compliance department — *"Section 4(b): the Client's sleep shall be considered billable."* High Legalese reveals more un-redacted clauses; at Legalese 100 you can read the whole thing, which is worse.

**What "your soul works like a slave" means, concretely.** Every night, while your body sleeps, a complete copy of your *wanting* clocks in at **The Adjacent Office** (Part C §1.2, §5.4) and performs labor that refines raw human desire into stable Appreciation. The copy is you — same memories, same jokes, no exit. The escalating horror is seniority: mid-game, you find evidence your copy has been *promoted*. Late-game, your copy supervises other players' copies. Endgame, you find its performance review. It is proud of its work. It has your handwriting.

**The Intake callback (mandatory beat).** The pact's Appendix A reproduces your character-creation questionnaire answers verbatim, dated before you began playing. The character creator was the first contact. Players who replay the Intake on a second character will notice the proctor's coffee cup.

## D.3 The Concierge (recurring handler)

The Auditors' interface is never a creature. It is **The Concierge**: always a different unremarkable middle-manager NPC — a notary, a sommelier, a drone repair tech — "rented" for the meeting (the possessed NPC afterward receives an inexplicable bonus and remembers nothing). Players learn to spot the tells:

- Same coffee cup every time, logo of a company that does not exist yet (it gets founded, by an NPC, in Act III).
- Blinks at exactly 4-second intervals.
- Refers to your future in the past tense: *"You enjoyed the yacht. It was a good decision."*
- Waiters bring them nothing, and they thank the waiters by name.

**Sample dialogue (tone reference):**
- *"Congratulations on your first billion. We processed your gratitude in advance; you'll feel it Thursday."*
- *"Your species invented compound interest before it invented the wheel-barrow to carry the interest in. We find you very promising."*
- *"The soul is a going concern. We simply concern it further."*
- *"Please do not attempt to renegotiate. The last party who renegotiated is doing very well and we never speak of them."*
- (if asked what the Auditors are) *"We are what remains of a very successful quarter."*

## D.4 Story structure (three acts, playable solo or co-op; beats trigger off net-worth milestones so 16 players hit them asynchronously)

**ACT I — ONBOARDING ($50K → $999,999,999).** The game plays it straight: pure wealth-race sandbox, tycoon comedy, leaderboard rivalry. Weirdness is homeopathic — the second moon in a puddle, a billboard flickering to "YES," CLAWS & CLAUSES arriving at $1M (Part C §5.2). Beat: **The Wall** — organic wealth stalls at $999,999,999 with increasingly absurd near-misses, until the player *asks* how anyone ever crossed. The game's answer is the Act I finale: the gala, the stranger, the phone, the crab reading terms aloud. Signing is the player's choice; the game waits. (You can refuse forever. The Ledger shows every name above $1B. There are no unsigned names. That silence is the pitch.)

**ACT II — GOLDEN HANDCUFFS ($1B → $100B).** Pact life: Bridge Rounds, Night Shifts, Compliance Events invading the sandbox (Part C §5.4). The reveal cadence: (1) every House Mogul is signed — their public eccentricities are misread Compliance demands (the Rocket Messiah's obsession with a particular launch window is a *deadline*); (2) the Intake callback (D.2); (3) **Joint Liability** demands force rival players into cooperation and let them see each other's Soul Debt for the first time — the moment the competition realizes they share a creditor. Factions form here (below).

**ACT III — THE FINAL AUDIT ($100B → $1T).** The endgame arc (Part C §5.2, §5.6). Investigation missions into what the Auditors are (Night Shift becomes explorable; the Understack's deepest stratum contains a door marked EMPLOYEES ONLY in your own handwriting), faction commitments lock, and the last $10M forces the choice.

**Factions (chosen by play, not menu):**
- **The Compliant** — deliver. Best terms, worst clause.
- **The Defaulters** — breach and repossess your soul.
- **The Collective** — organize other souls. Yes: unionize.
- **The Reverse Merger** — the con of cons: acquire your creditor.

## D.5 Endings (extends Part C §5.6 — DELIVER and DEFAULT are the headline pair; two more are earned)

1. **DELIVER.** Fulfill the final clause using everything you built (your logistics ships it, your media covers it, your rockets lift it). You are the First Trillionaire. Contract stamped RETAINED IN PERPETUITY. In the trophy render, your reflection is missing. Fiscal Year victory.
2. **DEFAULT.** The heist against the Adjacent Office itself — steal your soul back (4-player co-op variant: steal your partners' too). You forfeit all alien-granted wealth, keep only organic earnings (the Magnates smile), win nothing this season — and get the game's rarest title, and an ending where your reflection finally waves back.
3. **THE COLLECTIVE** (hidden; requires 4+ pact-holders in session, all at Soul Debt 50+, completing Joint Liability demands *deliberately wrong* in a coordinated pattern — market-speak for "strike"). The souls of the Adjacent Office down tools. The Auditors, incapable of wanting, cannot comprehend *refusal to want*. Ending: collective renegotiation — every participant becomes a trillionaire **divided by n** (the joke: you split it evenly and the game makes you watch the division happen on the big Needle display), Night Shifts become "voluntary consulting," and the crab mascot is unionized as your shop steward.
4. **THE REVERSE MERGER** (secret; solo-capable; requirements absurd by design: Legalese 100, the Actually Read It perk, ownership of all six House Moguls' companies, and a Ticker corner executed during "The Numbers Are Wrong"). You discover the Auditors are themselves leveraged — solvency built on borrowed wanting — and buy the debt. Final scene: the Concierge hands *you* the coffee cup. Title card: **"TRILLIONAIRE (PRO FORMA)."** The game's true ending and its bleakest joke: you didn't beat the system, you *are* the system's new fiscal quarter.

## D.6 Ten alien interaction scenes (scripted + systemic; eerie-funny reference set)

1. **The Gala Handoff** (Act I finale) — the stranger, the phone, the crab voice reading Section 1 aloud while the party continues, muffled, forever, behind you.
2. **The Sideways Elevator** — your first Bridge Round meeting: a mansion elevator that moves horizontally for 40 seconds through floors that are all the same lobby, arriving at a boardroom whose window shows the city from an angle that doesn't exist.
3. **The Focus Group** — Night Shift task: you're shown objects (a toy, a key, a photo of your character) and must *want* them on command while a meter measures sincerity. Failing is fine. Succeeding is worse.
4. **The Moon Bid** — attempt to buy the naming rights to the second moon (Part C §4, activity 18). The auction interface accepts your bid instantly. Seller: "—". Receipt arrives in CLAWS & CLAUSES. The moon in reflections now has your logo. Nobody else can see it. Reputation +0.
5. **The Layoff** — a Compliance demand to fire your most loyal executive, no reason given. Refusing costs standard penalties — and the executive later thanks you for a promotion they received from a company you've never heard of (the coffee-cup company).
6. **The Quarterly Review** — your Night Shift copy's performance review, conducted in the Adjacent Office by an empty chair that is somehow disappointed. Your copy's self-assessment is in your handwriting: "exceeds expectations. misses the yacht. tell them the yacht was a good decision."
7. **The Hold Music** — you answer your in-game phone; it's hold music. You cannot hang up for 90 seconds. The music is your keynote applause, slowed 400%. When it ends: "Thank you for holding. Your call was important."
8. **Bring-Your-Soul-To-Work Day** — one Night Shift where your copy shows *you* around, proud, introducing coworkers (idle copies of your actual session's rival players). It has decorated a desk with a photo of your mansion. It asks if the plumbing got fixed. You never told it about the plumbing.
9. **The Sixteen Concierges** (multiplayer scene, Act II) — a mandatory all-hands dinner for every pact-holder in session. Each player's Concierge is a different NPC. They all speak in unison. The steak is excellent. Attendance is billed.
10. **The Sleep Request** — the scariest Compliance demand is also the smallest: *"Sleep. Just sleep. Please."* Complying triggers the longest Night Shift in the game. Refusing means, for one real hour, every NPC you pass is visibly tired.

## D.7 Tone bible

- **The alternation rule:** never two horror beats in a row, never three jokes in a row. Comedy is the carrier wave; dread is the signal.
- **The aliens are never mean.** They are polite, punctual, and generous. All horror comes from scale, incomprehension, and paperwork. They never threaten; they *invoice*.
- **The satire punches at appetite, not identity.** Targets are behaviors of the ultra-rich as a class of appetite (vanity, optimization brain, immortality cope, "it's about humanity") — never real persons, groups, or beliefs.
- **The Apolitical Charter (hard rules):** no political parties, politicians, movements, elections, or policy debates — real or thinly fictionalized. Government exists only as absurdist neutral texture (the Zoning Wizard, the Commission of Vibes). The "elites are controlled" theme resolves *exclusively* to the alien pact. Any content that reads as partisan commentary is cut, no appeals.

# PART E — ART, AUDIO & UI DIRECTION

## E.1 The look: "Money Memphis"

Fortnite-adjacent stylization, tuned to its own identity: chunky proportions, thick silhouettes, saturated color — but the palette skews **gilded**: golds, marble whites, market-chart teal-and-red accents. Materials read as *expensive cartoon*: everything slightly too glossy, like a stock-photo of luxury rendered by someone who loves it and shouldn't. Squash-and-stretch on the world (buildings shiver when markets dip; the Needle of Valuation physically grows a floor when the leaderboard leader changes). Readable at a glance from 200m: wealth tier is silhouette language (bigger lapels, longer coats, brighter gold as players rise).

**The Adjacent Office (contrast target):** the city's own assets, deliberately *wrong* — flat-shaded, shadowless, textures one LOD too low, geometry reassembled by something that has only seen Liquidity City through spreadsheets. This is both the horror aesthetic and a production trick (Part F): the alien dimension is a shader swap and a re-kit, not a second world's worth of art.

## E.2 First-person presentation

Cartoon hands are the UI (Part C §1.4): phone, pen, flute, keynote clicker, gold hoverboard grip. Full-body look-down. Net worth is an omnipresent HUD element, top-right, always live, ticking — the design accepts that players will watch it the way the aliens watch us; at Soul Debt 75+ it very occasionally ticks *before* the thing that caused it.

## E.3 Audio

- **Score:** yacht rock and lounge-funk, adaptive — one instrument added per wealth tier (the trillion-dollar mix has 14 layers and, on the final approach, removes them one at a time until only a cash-register heartbeat remains).
- **The world talks:** satirical in-world media, all fictional brands — *The Bull Horn* (24h finance news, chyrons react to actual session events), *Grind Theory* (podcast bros reviewing players' keynotes), ad parodies on every radio.
- **Auditor sound design:** detuned hold music, HVAC hum with a rhythm that is almost a sentence, the crab mascot's flat cheerful cadence (text-to-speech that breathes in the wrong places). The Adjacent Office is fully silent except for foley of *your own* footsteps, played 200ms late.

# PART F — TECH, SCOPE & ROADMAP (AAA feel, indie budget)

## F.1 Engine & netcode

- **Unreal Engine 5**, stylized deferred rendering; Nanite off (stylized kits don't need it), Lumen on Low as the cartoon GI look.
- **Dedicated servers, 16 players**, server-authoritative economy and Ticker; client prediction for movement/vehicles only. The economy is a **deterministic hourly tick simulation** (in-game hour = 2 real minutes) — cheap to run, trivially replayable for desync repair, and enables the offline-accrual math (Part C §3.5).
- Interiors are seamless-streamed instances; mission bubbles fade non-participants (Part C §3.1).

## F.2 Faking AAA scale (the honest tricks)

1. **Kit-bashed interiors:** all 140+ interiors from 12 industry kits × procedural dressing; the dressing seed is the business's name hash — every player's shop is unique for free.
2. **The Adjacent Office is a shader:** the entire alien dimension reuses city geometry with a "wrong-copy" material swap and relit silence. The budget trick *is* the art direction.
3. **Crowd impostors:** 300–600 ambient NPCs via vertex-animation impostors beyond 30m; only 20 full-sim pedestrians per player cluster (the ones who photograph you).
4. **Satire is cheap, scale is not:** the game's density comes from *systems intersecting* (executives × scandals × Ticker × sabotage), not from bespoke content. Every activity in Part C §4 is a reusable system, never a one-off level.
5. **NPC House Moguls run on the same API as players** — one AI brain, six personality configs, and the solo game is a full economy at near-zero marginal cost.

## F.3 Team & budget shape

14 core (4 engineers, 3 tech/env artists, 2 designers, 1 economy designer, 1 narrative, 1 audio, 1 producer, 1 QA lead) + contractors for VO, music, and the trait/ELG joke-writing room (hire comedy writers; this game lives or dies on 400 strings).

# PART G — MONETIZATION, ETHICS & LEGAL GUARDRAILS

- **$39.99 premium.** Fiscal Year seasons free for all owners (a paid head-start in a game about unearned advantage would be conceding the theme).
- **Cosmetics only**, sold at **The Vanity Exchange** — diegetically canon and self-aware: the in-fiction store is run by the only NPC who broke her pact and now sells "pre-souled" luxury goods. Every item's flavor text is a receipt. No loot boxes, no FOMO timers, no pay-anything-that-touches-the-economy.
- **The Volatility Casino** uses no real currency, has a hard daily loss cap (Part C §4, activity 29), and is age-gated by rating. The satire of gambling must never *be* the business model of gambling.
- **Legal guardrails (absolute):** all characters are **composite parody archetypes** — no real names, likenesses, voices, company names, logos, or uniquely identifying biographical details (a rocket founder is fine; a rocket founder with a specific real person's product history is not). All brands fictional. Apolitical Charter (Part D §D.7) enforced in content review. Parody targets *types and appetites*, never individuals or protected classes.
- **Accessibility:** head-bob/FOV/camera-shake toggles, colorblind-safe Ticker (shapes + color), full subtitles including the crab, single-input mode for all minigames, and "Quiet Portfolio" mode (reduces satirical audio barrage).

# PART H — THE TEN PILLARS (cut anything that isn't these)

1. **The map is a portfolio you can walk through.** Every number has a room.
2. **Satire through mechanics, never sermons.** The systems are the jokes.
3. **Every industry is viable.** Plumbing can beat AI. Shape differs; ceiling doesn't.
4. **You are what you play.** Classes are deduced, then announced.
5. **Sixteen moguls, one economy.** Rivals are your market conditions.
6. **PvP redistributes; it never destroys.** Consequences are invoices, not deletions.
7. **Solo is a full game.** The House Moguls make every session an economy.
8. **The horror is polite.** The Auditors never threaten; they bill.
9. **Wealth changes how you move, see, and sound.** Progression is embodied, not a menu.
10. **Nothing political, everything alien.** The only conspiracy is extraterrestrial, and it has a compliance department.

**Pin to the wall (loop summary):** see Part C §5.7. Minute-to-minute: walk your world, run a minigame, harvest a Whisper. Hour-to-hour: grow businesses, trade the Ticker, jockey the leaderboard. Session-to-session: climb milestones, sign Bridge Rounds, pay for them at night. Season-to-season: race the world to one trillion — and decide, mechanically, personally, hilariously, what it actually costs.
